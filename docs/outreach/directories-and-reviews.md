## 1. Client reviews — send these today

Reviews on Clutch and GoodFirms are what AI assistants quote when someone asks
"who should build my website", and they carry a link. Ask only clients whose
work genuinely went well. Three is plenty to start: **Lighthouse Classes,
Kosmo Dental Clinic, Flaming Integrated Logistiks.**

Clutch verifies reviews, sometimes by a short call with the client, so warn
them. It takes the client about ten minutes.

### WhatsApp / email to a client (short version)

> Hi [Name], a quick favour, no rush.
>
> I'm listing Kodinav on Clutch, the directory businesses use to check
> developers before hiring them. Reviews there have to come from real clients,
> so I can't write them myself.
>
> Would you be willing to leave a short one about [project]? It's a form, about
> ten minutes, and Clutch sometimes calls to verify it's genuine. Say exactly
> what you thought, including anything that could have gone better — an honest
> review is worth more to me than a glowing one.
>
> Here's the link: [paste your Clutch review link]
>
> Thank you either way, and shout if you need anything on the site.

### If they ask "what should I write?"

> Whatever's true. The questions Clutch asks are roughly: what was the project,
> why did you pick me, what was delivered, what was the result, and what could
> be improved. Two or three sentences each is plenty.

Do not offer discounts or payment for reviews. It breaks the platforms' rules,
and a review that reads as bought does more harm than no review.

## What not to do

- **No paid link packages, guest-post farms or "500 backlinks" gigs.** They are
  the one thing that can actively hurt a domain this young.
- **No fake reviews, and no reviews in exchange for discounts.**
- **No Google Business Profile for Hong Kong or Taiwan** without a real address
  there. Fake or virtual-office addresses get profiles suspended.
- **Don't publish more pages** while 46 sit uncrawled. Links first.
